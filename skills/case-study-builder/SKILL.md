# Case Study Builder

## Why case studies are the highest-leverage B2B asset
- Used in sales (closes deals), ABM (personalized to vertical), ads (testimonial creatives), SEO (industry pages), email nurture, webinars, social.
- One great case study, properly produced, can be sliced into 10+ assets.
- For service businesses with no demos, it's often the #1 conversion lever.

## The story arc (Situation → Challenge → Solution → Results, "SCSR")

| Block | Goal | Key questions to ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Situation** | Anchor the reader in the client's world | Industry, size, role, what they were doing before |
| **Challenge** | Make the pain real | What was at stake, what they tried before, why it failed |
| **Solution** | Show the work, not the product | What you implemented, in what order, what made it specific to them |
| **Results** | Prove impact | Hard numbers + soft outcomes (time saved, peace of mind, team morale) |

End with a forward-looking quote ("we're now doing X") to imply the relationship continues.

## Client interview script (45 min)

1. Walk me through your role and what your team is responsible for.
2. Before working with us, what was the situation? What goal/problem triggered the project?
3. What had you tried before? Why didn't it work?
4. How did you decide to work with us? What almost stopped you?
5. Walk me through the project — what we did, in what order.
6. What's different now, concretely? (Push for numbers — see "extracting numbers" below.)
7. What's the impact on your team / customers / business?
8. Who else in your industry should know about this?
9. (Optional) What would you say to someone in your shoes considering this?

**Record with explicit consent**, transcribe, then edit — never write a case study from memory.

## Extracting numbers when the client "doesn't have data"

Rarely true — they have data, they just don't see it as data. Probe:
- **Time**: how many hours/week was this taking? How many meetings? How many emails?
- **People**: how many people were involved? At what cost rate?
- **Errors / rework**: how often did X go wrong? What did one error cost?
- **Speed**: how long did the cycle take? How long now?
- **Volume**: leads/month, deals/month, tickets/month — before vs after
- **Pipeline**: deals influenced, average deal size, win rate
- **Sentiment**: NPS, eNPS, retention, "would you recommend"

If still no number: ask for a **range** ("between 20 and 30%") or a **comparative** ("at least 2x faster"). Vague but truthful beats invented.

## Headline and subhead

The headline carries the proof. Best forms:
- "[Company] [outcome] in [timeframe] with [your category]"
  → "Alstom cut PRAP onboarding time by 40% in 6 months with PGC Formation"
- "How [company] [achieved outcome] without [common objection]"
  → "How Cora trained 280 employees on workplace safety without disrupting store operations"

Subhead = one-sentence summary of the SCSR arc.

## Layout (one-pager)

```
[Logo]   [Industry] · [Company size] · [Region]

HEADLINE (the result)
Subhead (one sentence)

Pull quote from champion (1-2 sentences, with name + role)

CHALLENGE (3-4 bullets or short paragraph)
SOLUTION (3-4 bullets)
RESULTS (3-5 metrics in big numbers)

Closing quote / forward-looking statement

[Logo]   [CTA: "See how we can help you" → demo/audit link]
```

## Length variants from one interview

| Asset | Length | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Headline + 3 metrics | 1 line | Sales deck, email signature, ad |
| LinkedIn post | 150–300 words | Organic social, founder's profile |
| One-pager PDF | 1 page | Sales handoff, BOFU email |
| Full case study | 1000–1500 words | Website, SEO, ABM landing page |
| Video testimonial | 60–90 sec | Ads, landing page hero, webinar |
| Long-form story / podcast | 20–40 min | Authority play, top of funnel |

## Quote selection

Best quotes are:
- **Specific** ("we cut accidents from 14/year to 3/year") not "they were great"
- **Emotional** ("I finally sleep at night")
- **Anti-objection** ("I was worried about [common fear] but…")
- **Said by a senior buyer**, not the user (more weight)

Cut filler words, fix grammar lightly, but **never invent words**.

## Approvals workflow

1. Send draft to interviewee for content review
2. Loop in their legal/comms if it's a large enterprise (always for Fortune 500 / regulated industries)
3. Get explicit written approval to use logo + name + quote
4. Specify usage scope (website, sales materials, ads — be broad)
5. Offer a soft-launch where they can co-promote on their LinkedIn

If logo/name approval is blocked: anonymize ("a leading French BTP firm with 3,000 employees") — still useful with category proof.

## Reuse playbook

For each finished case study, schedule:
- 1 LinkedIn post from founder
- 1 LinkedIn post from sales lead
- 1 client newsletter feature
- 3 sales sequence touches (cold + nurture + close)
- 1 paid ad creative (testimonial)
- 1 page on the website with SEO-targeted URL (`/case-studies/{client}-{outcome}`)
- 1 ABM personalization snippet for similar accounts in the same vertical

## Common mistakes
- Writing it like a product page (features) instead of a story (transformation)
- No numbers, only adjectives
- Quoting the user instead of the buyer
- One asset, one use — instead of slicing into 10
- Not updating quotes/numbers when the relationship grows
- Asking "are you happy with us?" instead of digging for transformation
