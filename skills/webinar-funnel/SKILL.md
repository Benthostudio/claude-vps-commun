# Webinar Funnel

## When a webinar works (and when it doesn't)
**Works for**: B2B with consultative sale, complex product, high ticket, regulated/educational topic, audiences that learn before they buy.
**Doesn't work for**: impulse purchases, audiences that already know the topic, very small TAM, when you can't deliver real value in 30–45 min.

## Funnel structure

```
Promotion → Registration page → Confirmation + reminder sequence
   → Live (or evergreen) webinar → Pitch + CTA
   → Attendees nurture / No-show recovery → Sales handoff
```

## Topic selection

The topic is the funnel. Three rules:
1. **Pain-led title**: "How to [achieve outcome] without [common objection]" beats "Introduction to [product category]".
2. **Specific to one persona**: not "marketing leaders" but "HSE managers in industrial sites".
3. **Implies a system, not a tip**: "The 3-step [Name] framework" converts better than "5 tips for X".

## Registration page essentials

- Headline = exact promise (the outcome attendee will get)
- 3–5 bullet "what you'll learn" (specific, mechanism-revealing)
- Speaker block with credibility (logos, results)
- Date / time / duration with timezone
- Single primary CTA, no nav, no distractions
- Social proof (past attendee quotes, "847 already registered")

Target: **30–50% conversion rate** on warm traffic, 10–20% on cold.

## Reminder sequence (live webinar)

| When | Channel | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate | Email + ICS calendar | Confirm + add to calendar |
| 24h before | Email | Excitement + "what to bring" |
| 1h before | Email + SMS (optional) | Final reminder + link |
| 5 min before | Email | "We're starting" |
| Mid-webinar | (live) | "Stay till the end for [bonus]" |

Show-up benchmark: **30–50% of registrants** for live webinars.

## Slide structure (Perfect Webinar — adapted)

1. **Hook** (5 min) — bold promise, agenda, "stay till the end for X"
2. **Origin story** (5 min) — credibility + relatability
3. **Core teaching** (20–25 min) — the framework / 3 secrets / case studies
4. **Transition** (3 min) — natural bridge from teaching to offer
5. **Pitch** (10 min) — what you offer, who it's for, what's included, price, scarcity
6. **Q&A** (10 min) — handles objections live, repeats CTA

**80/20 rule**: 80% teaching, 20% pitch. Pitching too early kills trust.

## Pitch mechanics

- Stack the offer (deliverables + bonuses) before revealing price
- Anchor against the cost of inaction or a competing solution
- Time-bound bonus or price (only if true)
- Single, simple CTA — one URL, one action

## Live vs evergreen

| | Live | Evergreen |
|---|---|---|
| Effort | High per session | High once, then low |
| Conversion | Higher (urgency, Q&A) | Lower but constant |
| Best for | Launches, top-of-funnel events | Always-on lead gen |
| Stack | Zoom + Riverside + StreamYard | EverWebinar, WebinarFuel, Demio |

Often the best play: run live 2–3 times to refine, then turn the best into evergreen.

## Post-webinar follow-up (for attendees)

- Day 0: Replay link + recap email + CTA
- Day 1: Case study or testimonial reinforcing the offer
- Day 3: Objection-handling email (price, time, fit)
- Day 5: Last-call email with deadline
- Day 7: Break-up email + invite to next webinar / lower commitment offer

## No-show recovery

No-shows are warmer than cold leads but cooler than attendees.
- Day 0: "Sorry we missed you, here's the replay"
- Day 2: Highlight clip (2–3 min) of the best moment
- Day 4: CTA email with same offer

## KPIs

| Stage | Metric | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Promotion | Registration page conversion | 30–50% warm / 10–20% cold |
| Show-up | Attendees / registrants | 30–50% live |
| Engagement | Avg watch time / drop-off curve | > 60% watch through pitch |
| Pitch | Offer click rate | 5–15% of attendees |
| Sales | Webinar → opportunity | 1–5% of registrants |
| Revenue | Cost per registrant vs ACV | Depends on ACV |

## Common mistakes
- Too much teaching, no pitch (no revenue)
- Too much pitch, no teaching (no trust)
- Generic topic (low registration)
- No reminder sequence (40% no-show)
- No replay strategy (leaves money on the table)
- Asking for a meeting too early in the pitch (better: low-friction next step like a free audit)
